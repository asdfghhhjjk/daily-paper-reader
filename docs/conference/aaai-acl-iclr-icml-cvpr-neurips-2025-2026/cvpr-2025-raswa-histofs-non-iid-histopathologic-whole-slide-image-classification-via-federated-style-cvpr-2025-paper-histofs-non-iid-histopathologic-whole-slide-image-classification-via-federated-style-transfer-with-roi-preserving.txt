# Abstract 

Federated learning for pathological whole slide image (WSI) classification allows multiple clients to train a global multiple instance learning (MIL) model without sharing their privacy-sensitive WSIs. To accommodate the non-independent and identically distributed (non-i.i.d.) feature shifts, cross-client style transfer has been popularly used but is subject to two fundamental issues: (1) WSI contains multiple morphological structures, each corresponding to a distinct style. (2) Performing style transfer may potentially shift the region of interests (RoIs) in the augmented WSIs. To address these challenges, we propose HistoFS, a feder-ated learning framework for computational pathology on non-i.i.d. feature shifts in WSI classification. Specifically, we introduce pseudo bag styles that capture multiple style variations within a single WSI. In addition, an authenticity module is introduced to ensure that RoIs are preserved, al-lowing local models to learn WSIs with diverse styles while maintaining essential RoIs. Extensive experiments vali-date the superiority of HistoFS over state-of-the-art meth-ods on three clinical datasets. Our code is available at 

https://lalakitchen.github.io/HistoFS/ .

# 1. Introduction 

1.1. Background 

Histopathology Whole Slide Image (WSI) classification plays a crucial role in computational pathology by automat-ing disease diagnosis [5, 6]. Since WSIs are gigabyte-sized images that capture a wide range of cellular and tissue struc-tures, the Multiple Instance Learning (MIL) model [7–10] is well-suited for this task by treating a WSI as a bag con-taining thousands of smaller “patch” images. Furthermore, the pseudo-bag concepts [11–13] are introduced to partition thousands of patch features into smaller bags. On the other hand, to enable clients/institutions to collaboratively train a global model without sharing sensitive WSI data, HistoFL [3] and PointTransformerFL [4] introduce federated learn-

So, augmenting local data through cross-client style transfer ( e.g. , CCST [21] and StableFDG [22]) has become a popular paradigm for addressing non-i.i.d. feature shifts within FL training. For ex-ample, WSI patches exhibit multiple morphologies corre-sponding to non-uniform statistical properties [25]. Furthermore, transmitting thousands of styles per WSI will incur high communica-tion costs in a federated setting. • Second, prior MIL works [7–10] have shown that re-gion of interests (RoIs) occupy a small, randomly dis-tributed portion of a WSI (bag). Therefore, localizing RoIs or patch features that contribute to the WSI is essen-tial. However, the statistical properties ( e.g. , mean and standard deviation) of patch features are calculated glob-ally [28, 29], without considering the local context [4, 10] and correlation information [7, 9] between patch features. Thus, augmenting local WSI through style transfer will change the RoIs, leading to sub-optimal performance. 

1.2. Contribution 

We propose HistoFS, a novel federated learning method for classification of WSIs with non-i.i.d. features in a federated MIL paradigm. It is mainly composed of two key strategies. 

Pseudo Bag Styles. Before federated learning model train-ing, we first compute the multiple distinct styles, known as “pseudo bag styles,” encapsulated within each WSI (bag) for each client. The clients/institutions then upload their pseudo bag styles to the global server for further sharing with all the institutions. After that, each institution uses the shared styles transmitted from the server to augment its lo-cal WSI (bag). Notably, this augmentation only applies to the bag of patch features, so we do not reconstruct stylized image, as in [21, 22]. Our pseudo bag strategy is also dif-ferent from [11–13] in that ours is style information aware instead of a random partition. 

Authenticity Module. With the pseudo-bag styles, the lo-cal MIL model can see as diverse styles as possible. How-ever, we find that augmenting local WSIs through style transfer suffers the risk of shifting RoIs. Thus, we propose an authenticity module to preserve RoIs by measuring and aligning the cosine similarity of attention weights between the local and augmented WSIs. These attention weights can be viewed as expert annotations that identify patch features’ contributions to WSI classification. This enables us to train a more generalized local MIL model, improving global MIL model performance. Our key contributions are summarized as follows: • In contrast to existing federated methods that focus on style transfer of images with a single morphological struc-ture, our method focuses on style transfer of WSIs with multiple morphological structures. (Sec. 4.1.1) • To our knowledge, recent studies for whole slide images in the federated setting only focus on employing the ag-gregation functions, neglecting the investigation of non-i.i.d. feature shifts among institutions. We reveal this is-sue and propose HistoFS to solve it. (Sec. 4.1.3) • We introduce an authenticity module to preserve RoIs of a WSI, enabling the global MIL model to better benefit from cross-client pseudo bag styles transfer. (Sec. 4.1.4) 

# 2. Related Works 

2.1. MIL for WSIs 

Multiple Instance learning [7–10] has been widely used to classify whole slide images (WSIs). It consists of three steps: 1) Each WSI is divided into smaller patches, 2) Fea-tures are extracted from individual patches using a pre-trained encoder, and 3) Patch features are aggregated to predict slide-level label. The earlier MIL aggregator was designed using handcrafted max- and mean-pooling opera-tors [36, 37]. Nevertheless, a WSI is composed of normal tissue and region of interests (RoIs), where RoIs can be ei-ther tumors or abnormal tissues. To localize RoIs, advanced methodologies, including ABMIL [7], CLAM [8], DSMIL [9], and FRMIL [10], are proposed. In response to heterogeneous tissue structure, pseudo-bags are introduced, dividing a single bag into smaller bags. DTFD-MIL [11] proposed randomly partitioning one bag into pseudo-bags. PseMix [12] proposed a prototype-based clustering to constrain the random pseudo bag assignment. PAMIL [13] proposed dynamic policy-driven patch selec-tion for pseudo-bag alignment. 

2.2. Federated Learning for WSIs 

Federated Learning ( e.g. , FedAvg [38], HistoFL [3], and PointTransformerFL [4]) aggregates the information trans-mitted from multiple clients/institutions to train a shared global model without needing their actual/private data. To address the issue of feature shifts across different in-stitutions, FedBN [39] introduced “feature normalization,” while FedProx [40] and FedRCL [41] introduced “feature regularization” for natural images. In medical imaging, HarmoFL [33] introduced weight perturbation in the global model and amplitude normalization locally. Shen et al. [34] proposed a federated GAN for stain normalization, while Zhang et al. [35] suggested generating pseudo images for centralized training. Yan et al. [42] introduced federated self-supervised learning to extract non-i.i.d. features. Nev-ertheless, employing federated learning for whole slide im-age with non-i.i.d features remains relatively unexplored.

2.4. Differences from Prior Studies 

In Table 1, our HistoFS is compared with the methods of FL for WSIs and those of style transfer for natural images in FL in terms of three characteristics, including “Feature shifts,” “Multiple morphologies of input,” and “Authentic-ity of augmented input.” We note that only HistoFS can possess the three characteristics: (1) We propose a pseudo bag styles construction strategy that encapsulates multiple distinct styles within a single WSI to address feature shift. These styles enable us to better model the diverse character-istics inherent in various morphological structures. (2) Dif-ferent from DACS [26], which preserves the authenticity of augmented data by minimizing the entropy score between local and global models, and FedFDA [32], which mini-mizes the variation between augmented and original data using a client-agnostic loss, our method treats the authen-ticity by preserving region of interests in augmented WSIs. This allows a local MIL model to learn local WSIs data with diverse styles as possible while keeping local context and correlation information between features inside WSIs. (3) Different from [33–35], HistoFS do not overlook the pres-ence of multiple morphologies and the potential loss of Re-gion of Interests (ROIs) when addressing feature shifts in medical imaging, particularly in WSIs. Moreover, different from the pseudo-bag MIL methods [11–13] that randomly partition the bag at the patch feature level without explicitly considering the characteristics of the tissue regions, our strategy divides the bag from the per-spective of style information, preserving the feature charac-teristics of the specific region.

# 4. Proposed Method: HistoFS 

In response to the challenges discussed in Sec. 1, we will describe how the proposed method, HistoFS, comprehen-sively solves them. The notations frequently used are de-picted in Sec. 9 of Appendix for clarity. 

4.1. Rationale Behind HistoFS 

Federated learning allows us to train a MIL model collabo-ratively without transferring the WSIs from all participating institutions. However, as illustrated in Fig. 1, the patch fea-ture distributions of distinct WSI from different institutions are non-i.i.d. Our objective is to make these patch features as similar in distribution as possible by introducing styles from other institutions into each of them. In this way, our proposed HistoFS ensures that the trained local MIL models fit a wide range of styles and avoid local weights being bi-ased toward institution-specific styles. The overall process of HistoFS is illustrated in Fig. 2. In federated learning, training occurs in global rounds, denoted as t = 1 , 2, . . . , T . Without loss of generality, sometimes In will be omitted in the following descriptions for notation simplicity. The key components of HistoFS in-clude: (1) Pseudo Bag Styles Construction . We aim to capture multiple distinct styles to encapsulate the diverse statistical properties present in WSI (bag) (Sec. 4.1.1). (2) 

Server-Side Broadcasting . The server receives all pseudo bag styles from all institutions and shares these styles in a non-overlapping mechanism (Sec. 4.1.2). (3) Local Pseudo Bag Styles Transfer . Augment the local WSIs based on the pseudo bag styles transmitted from the server (Sec. 4.1.3). (4) Authenticity Module . Preserve RoIs by measuring and aligning the cosine similarity between the local and aug-mented WSIs (Sec. 4.1.4). These component work in com-plementary fashion to deal with non-i.i.d feature shifts in federated learning for whole slide image classification.

In addition to obtaining the model weights W by minimiz-ing a global cost function based on Eq. The server is then to send back these styles to the institutions for pseudo bag style transfer with-out redundancy in that the institution In receives pseudo bag styles from all the institutions other than itself. Concretely, it is beneficial to have multiple pseudo bag styles from dif-ferent WSIs to accommodate a diverse pseudo bag styles as possible. However, transmitting all pseudo bag styles can lead to high communication costs. To address this concern, we randomly select less than M In pseudo bag styles from institution In to form a set of pseudo bag styles that will be sent to the server.

5.2. Experiment Settings and Evaluation Metrics 

We compare HistoFS with state-of-the-art methods, includ-ing MIL-based [3, 10, 11], local style transfer [30, 31], and federated style transfer [21, 26]. The best model was se-lected based on the validation set and evaluated on test set. See Sec. 14 of Appendix for more detailed settings. All the hyper-parameters required in the methods se-lected for comparison come from the corresponding papers. It is important to note that since the local style transfer and federated style transfer methods are designed for patch-level images, we applied them to extract and transfer styles for random patch features on each WSI. Moreover, due to the concern of incompatibility, some methods listed in Ta-ble 1 were not selected for experimental comparison. For example, StableFDG [22] proposes to compute the spatial feature similarity between two different images, which is in-compatible with the scenario considered here as WSIs have different image sizes. PseMix [35] and PAMIL [13] share the similar concept with pseudo-bag; however, they focus on feature augmentation and selection, which are different from our purpose. As for evaluation metrics, the area under the curve (AUC), balanced accuracy (bACC), and F1-score were adopted. On the other hand, to further assess performance divergence that exists in a FL setting, we employ the en-tropy (H) score, defined as:

State-of-the-arts Comparison. The experiment results are depicted in Table 4. We have the following obser-vations: (1) Our HistoFS achieves state-of-the-art perfor-mance. Specifically, for the federated setting in the RCC and HER2 datasets, we achieve average AUC scores of

In addition, our method also outperforms lo-cal [30, 31] and federated style transfer [21, 26] methods, demonstrating its capability of using pseudo bag styles to extract and transfer multiple styles from a single whole slide image. (2) HistoFS outperforms other methods in the pseudo bag styles and authenticity module. Specifically, we experimented with the pseudo bag styles and authen-ticity module separately. Compared with MixStyle [30] and CCST [21], our proposed pseudo bag styles alone already achieve superior performance. Furthermore, integrating the authenticity module further improves our approach. (3) HistoFS is compatible with other MIL models. Specifically, when substituting the attention-gated aggregation (Eq. (3)) with DTFD-MIL [11] and FRMIL [10], the “average AUC” is nearly maintained, demonstrating the compatibility of HistoFS for WSI classification learning.

Communication Load and Average FL Training Time. 

In the federated setting, we conducted a total of 50 com-munication rounds, where each round involves two com-munication steps: uplink (sending data to the server) and downlink (receiving data from the server). Two types of data, including model weights and styles, with each param-eter occupying approximately 4 bytes, were transmitted. In particular, CCST [21] requires sharing thousands of style information of each patch feature per WSI in each com-munication round, and DACS [26] needs to transmit both the local and global models on each communication round to perform the authenticity loss. In contrast, our proposed HistoFS transmits K = 5 pseudo styles per WSI. Overall, as shown in Table 6, our method generally obtains a higher AUC (Table 4) but exhibits lower communication overhead and lower average training time per global round.

# 6. Conclusions 

We have presented a pseudo bag styles transfer and an au-thenticity module for whole slide image classification un-der the federated learning environment. The state-of-the-art performance of our proposed method is demonstrated on two diagnostic datasets with comprehensive experiments. Our study opens a new way for addressing non-i.i.d. WSIs to access diversified style distributions without sharing orig-inal WSIs, which helps avoid local models being biased to-ward institution-specific styles. Joint consideration of pre-serving privacy with HistoFS will be the future work. Last but not least, it is worth further studying if the issues consid-ered in this paper can be readily bypassed in the extensive foundation models.
